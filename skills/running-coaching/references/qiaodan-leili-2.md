## Construction — separate claims from facts

### Foam and platform
Official Qiaodan material calls the midsole Q-KUNGFU PRO with a newly tuned **blended PEBA** formulation. Do not upgrade this to verified 100% PEBA; that wording appears with the distinct GT model.

Official material lists **44 mm total stack including the insole**, a wide max-cushion platform, and a 7 mm supercritical stability insole; the retailer separately lists 37 mm heel/30 mm forefoot and 7 mm drop. These use different inclusion conventions, so do not present 37/30 as directly equivalent to the official full-stack figure or infer a precise full-shoe forefoot stack. Treat dimensions as manufacturer/retailer specifications, not independent measurements.

Reference mass is **228 g** in official material, but the reference size is not clearly established there. An independent user reports left 275 g/right 276 g in US 13. Size dependence prevents direct ranking against US 9 masses or John's unweighed pair.

Neither the official construction graphics nor the retailer specification lists a propulsion plate or rods. Treat the standard Leili 2.0 as an unplated cushioning-led trainer unless John's exact pair/SKU proves otherwise. Never confuse CPU outsole material with a plate.

### Geometry, upper, outsole
Retailer claims widened forefoot/midfoot base, 117.5/78/90 mm forefoot/midfoot/heel widths, and a more pronounced rolling geometry than v1. Dimensions lack an independently verified size/sample. A wider base can help stability, but outsole width does not establish internal toe-room or make this a clinical stability shoe.

Retailer describes breathable supportive woven upper and a 7 mm supercritical 'stability' insole. Cached Runner's Verdict description says single-layer perforated jacquard mesh, a somewhat narrow forefoot, and rubber coverage concentrated in wear zones with exposed foam. These are secondary descriptions, not direct observations of John's pair; test toe splay, forefoot compression, heel slip, lace pressure and hotspots rather than prescribing size from marketing.

Independent user search excerpt describes CPU outsole with good initial grip but explicitly says wet conditions were not tested. No dependable wet-grip or lifespan claim can be made. Inspect exposed foam and high-wear contact zones over time; do not promise a replacement distance from this evidence.

### Ride and marketing limits
Available early impressions describe soft/squishy, springy high-stack cushioning; intended use centers on easy and longer daily mileage, with steady/tempo versatility claimed by the retailer. Retailer's 84% energy return, 12% impact reduction and G-value 6.1 are unverified marketing/test claims. They are NOT John's running-economy benefit, proof of reduced Achilles load, or injury-risk reduction. Advertised pace ranges are not biomechanical eligibility thresholds.

## Fit into John's rotation

Mission hypotheses, not a controlled personal ranking:
- Red Hare 9 Ultra: closest existing long/easy-to-steady overlap, with BOOM/SUPER BOOM and PROBAR LOC support. Leili may offer an alternative soft ride; superiority is untested.
- Vomero Plus: other main overlap, full ZoomX, no plate, nominal 10 mm drop. Leili's nominal 7 mm is a geometry change; softer or lighter does not mean safer or better recovery.
- Evo SL: Lightstrike Pro and small shank, no rods; established versatile progression/performance option. Leili is provisionally on the cushioned easy side, with faster use to be earned.
- Boston 13: dual foam plus glass-fibre EnergyRods, established structured fast-training option. Do not call those a full carbon plate.
- Red Hare 9 Pro: BOOM/PROBAR LOC; John's historical report describes it as firmer/less forgiving than Ultra. Keep available for controlled daily or faster work.
- Kinvara 15/16: unplated PWRRUN, flexible, low-stack and 4 mm drop. Different ground feel/loading, not inherently disqualified from tempo.
- Streakfly 2: ZoomX plus full carbon Flyplate and aggressive low-stack geometry for short race-specific speed.
- Flyknit Lunar 3: historical Achilles warning dominates; avoid-for-now unless deliberately reassessed. Still marked active in the DB, which does not erase the caution.

## Onboarding and future evaluation

1. First check current Achilles/calf/foot symptoms. If at usual settled baseline and already tolerating running, use a short 20–30 minute genuinely easy trial on a familiar flat surface. This is a shoe-tolerance protocol, not an order to add training or override Garmin Coach; check live plan/readiness before selecting the actual day.
2. No first-run long test, hills or maximal speed. Stop for sharp or escalating pain or altered gait. A softer shoe does not authorize more load.
3. Record comfort on the current 1–5 scale, fit/hotspots, heel lockdown, lateral stability, cushioning/response and any asymmetry. Record during-run pain and the next-morning Achilles/calf/foot response; missing symptoms are not confirmed absence.
4. Increase exposure only after repeated tolerable responses. One run is anecdotal, not a permanent promotion to long-run duty.
5. For a later comparison against Ultra or Vomero, use repeated matched treadmill/route runs with comparable cooling, recovery, duration and source-verified Stryd power. Check late-run HR/mechanics and subjective tolerance. Do not merge Garmin-only watts into Stryd comparisons or equate model-derived power with measured physiological economy.
6. Query current counts/mileage and observations each time. At onboarding there were zero linked runs and zero observations; that is not a claim the physical pair has never been worn.
