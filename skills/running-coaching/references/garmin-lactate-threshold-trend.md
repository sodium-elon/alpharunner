# Garmin Lactate-Threshold Trend Analysis

Use this procedure when John shares Garmin's Running Lactate Threshold chart or asks what its trend means.

## Core rule

Treat chart points as **sparse algorithmic estimates**, not daily measurements or direct laboratory observations. The connecting line only joins accepted updates. Never interpret it before matching each marker to the underlying activity.

## Required workflow

1. **Read both axes separately.** Red is threshold heart rate; blue is threshold pace. Record approximate values and image-resolution uncertainty.
2. **Locate marker dates from x-position.** A marker may sit between labeled weekly ticks. Do not assign it the nearest printed tick without checking its relative position.
3. **Cross-match AlphaRunner.** Query runs on the inferred date and adjacent dates when the marker lies between ticks. Verify Garmin Activity ID, workout intent, HR source, duration, pace, HR, and laps.
4. **Inspect workout structure.** Distinguish continuous threshold-like running from sprints, intervals, short finishes, recoveries, and treadmill sessions. A short fast repetition can produce an impressive-looking threshold estimate without demonstrating sustained threshold ability.
5. **Check data integrity.** Flag impossible or inconsistent treadmill combinations such as summary pace conflicting with best pace, duration, lap distance, or lap pace. Do not use corrupted pace fields as physiological evidence.
6. **Compare like-for-like lap blocks.** For continuous runs, compare equal early and late blocks after excluding warm-up, cooldown, and tiny residual laps. Report pace and HR together. Quantify whether HR rose while pace stayed flat or slowed.
7. **Audit HR provenance per candidate run.** Use AlphaRunner's recorded `hr_source`; do not generalize from the runner's overall strap usage. State which detections came from chest strap versus optical sensing.
8. **Grade evidence.** Give highest weight to sustained, steady chest-strap efforts with coherent pace/lap data. Give lower weight to optical-only, interval-shaped, sprint-shaped, short, or internally inconsistent runs.
9. **Separate observation from inference.** Say what Garmin plotted, what AlphaRunner recorded, and what coaching conclusion is inferred. Never call an algorithmic estimate a measured lactate threshold.

## Interpretation patterns

- **Faster pace plus similar HR from a sustained effort:** possible fitness improvement.
- **Slower pace plus lower estimated HR:** may indicate fatigue, heat, dehydration, illness, detraining, or algorithmic correction; determine which from the matched activity and recovery context.
- **Large one-point spike after sprints/intervals:** likely workout-structure contamination until confirmed by sustained running.
- **HR increase at unchanged/slower pace across comparable blocks:** cardiac decoupling; consider accumulated load and environmental/recovery factors.
- **Stable pace and HR across a long continuous block:** strong durability evidence and usually more trustworthy than a dramatic estimate generated by short repetitions.
- **No points after a date:** no accepted update, not proof of stagnation or decline.

## Output requirements

Lead with the matched pattern, not generic Garmin theory. Include:

- approximate chart point(s), with date uncertainty where relevant;
- matching activity date and Garmin Activity ID when available;
- HR source;
- workout structure;
- early-versus-late pace/HR evidence for continuous runs;
- a blunt verdict on which points are credible and which are contaminated.

Do not prescribe precise HR or pace zones from this chart alone. Require corroboration from sustained efforts or a controlled test.
