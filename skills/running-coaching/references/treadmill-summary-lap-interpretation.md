# Treadmill activity totals versus lap distances

## Evidence pattern

A treadmill import can have one recorded activity-summary distance while the lap distances sum to a different total over exactly the same duration. Compare the actual source totals with tools. Preserve the summary as the imported headline and distinguish it from independently verified physical distance. Explicit user confirmation of the recorded total and absent power is current context, not a reason for repeated investigation.

The failure to avoid is presenting the conflicting lap-derived total/pace as a competing whole-run verdict, or an 'imaginary speed' warning that unnecessarily challenges the accepted total. An internal numerical discrepancy is not sufficient evidence that the runner's total is wrong.

## Reusable handling

1. Check summary distance, summed lap distance and both durations with tools. Equal durations do not suggest missing run time.
2. Retain the summary as the recorded headline and preserve raw laps. Explicit user confirmation supports acceptance of the recorded total, not a claim of laboratory accuracy.
3. A final treadmill calibration/distance edit may explain unadjusted lap totals. Label this a hypothesis unless edit/calibration evidence is available. Do not infer whether distance came from the treadmill, watch accelerometer, or another sensor merely because Stryd was absent.
4. Localize uncertainty to uses of lap distance: repetition pace, distance per effort and pace-based comparison. A diagnostic alternate pace is not a replacement headline.
5. Use timed work/recovery counts and appropriately qualified within-bout mechanics. Shorter recorded sprint distances alone do not prove loss of speed when lap calibration is unresolved; stable cadence or rising GCT alone does not settle the cause either.
6. Accept explicitly absent power; do not turn it into an authentication or sensor investigation without conflicting evidence.

## Concise explanation

“The activity summary is the recorded distance and what I imported. Garmin's individual lap distances sum differently over the same duration. That can occur when a treadmill activity total is adjusted without rewriting every lap; I haven't verified that mechanism here. The caution concerns individual lap speeds, not replacing your run total.”

A clarification does not itself repair already-saved coaching. Keep conversational correction and persisted-note repair distinct; use an authorized supported repair path if requested, never reimport or silently mutate records to fix prose.
